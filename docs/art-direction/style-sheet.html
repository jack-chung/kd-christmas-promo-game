The cast

Supporting cast

Note-creature Tunies: round face, note stem, scarf, bright body. Backup singers: cranberry-and-gold costumes, gold earrings, cheerful silhouette.

No Santa-style red hats with white trim on any character or background Tunie. Use brimmed caps, beanies, earmuffs and scarves. The King wears royal purple and gold, with a crown and music-note trim; no red robe with white fur trim. Archived gallery concepts stay unchanged.

Grid, edges, and motion

  • Character master: 40 × 46 logical pixels. Gift master: 64 × 64. House master: 64 × 70. Draw with filled rectangles on integer coordinates.
  • Use hard stair-step contours and 1–3 logical-pixel ink edges. No smooth vector curves, gradients, photographic textures, or blended brushwork.
  • Keep each costume to one base and one shade, with a few cream highlights. Outlines remain dark plum rather than pure black.
  • Use the same sprite source in every screen. Prefer whole-number display scales; shrink only where mobile controls need space. Keep UI text in readable system fonts, with monospace headings.
  • Animate whole pieces: bow lift, lid lift, short pad squash, a gentle tower sway. Respect reduced motion. Keep puzzle targets still and beat cues readable.

What this prototype simplifies

The concept image’s dense town, dozens of background characters, elaborate fabric detail, and painterly shading become a restrained village, small front-facing sprites, and two-tone parcels. These are original production placeholders, not crops from the concept paintings. Detailed side/back poses and a large animation library are deferred until the main game is selected.

Keep every scene cheerful and spoiler-free. Use wrapping materials, town houses, show preparations, and the established cast. Musical artwork, lyrics, recordings, and story-resolution imagery never belong in this sprite library.

Source of truth: art.js for pixel geometry; style.css for presentation; content.js for names and copy.
